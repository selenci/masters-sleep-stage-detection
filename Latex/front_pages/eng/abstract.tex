\chapter*{Abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Title:} \ttitleEn
\bigskip

Sleep staging is a process of categorizing sleep recordings into distinct
sleep phases which is essential for diagnosing sleep disorders and assessing
neurological health. However, automated staging models often struggle to generalize
across age groups due to developmental shifts in electroencephalography (EEG) signals. In this thesis, we propose
and evaluate four transformer-based strategies for handling age: an unconditioned baseline,
explicit age conditioning, age-specific models, and adult-to-pediatric transfer learning.
Our models achieved peak accuracies of 76.8\% on adults, 83.6\% on teenagers, 76.2\% on children,
and 74.9\% on infants, outperforming the established clinical tool YASA, especially on younger cohorts.
